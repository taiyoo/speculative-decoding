\begin{abstract}
Speculative decoding is widely reported to improve large language model (LLM) latency, but practical
behavior on consumer RTX devices is highly sensitive to hardware profile,
runtime path, and decoding policy. We present a controlled study spanning two
model families (Qwen2.5 and Qwen3) and two consumer-GPU setups
(RTX4090 and RTX5090-laptop). For fixed-policy settings ($k\in\{4,8,16\}$;
deterministic/stochastic), the RTX4090 Qwen3 run family shows best speedup at
$k{=}4$, deterministic ($S{=}2.8548$) and monotonic degradation with larger
$k$ ($\bar{S}_{k=4}=2.6673$, $\bar{S}_{k=8}=1.8258$, $\bar{S}_{k=16}=1.0405$),
while acceptance efficiency and accepted block size move in opposite directions
for latency optimization. Across matched pairs, deterministic remains faster
than stochastic at every $k$ (mean gap $0.2633$ speedup units). On
RTX5090-laptop, the same ordering trends persist (best at $k{=}4$,
deterministic; lower at larger $k$; deterministic $>$ stochastic), but absolute
speedups are below 1 under the tested stack, highlighting a deployment-critical
portability gap. We additionally report seed-level variance (mean$\pm$std),
time to first token (TTFT), time per output token (TPOT), and corrected paired
tests to strengthen statistical transparency.
The Qwen2.5 results show the same qualitative low-$k$ and
deterministic-preference trends, providing cross-family consistency at the
trend level. These findings refine the paper's claim: this work provides reproducible,
deployment-oriented characterization and policy guidance rather than a
universally throughput-improving new algorithm.
\end{abstract}
