\section{Discussion on Adaptive Speculative Strategies Derived from Empirical Findings}
\label{sec:acsd}

The current empirical study identifies the strongest fixed-policy speedup at
smaller block size ($k{=}4$), with net speed degrading as $k$ increases even
when accepted block length rises. This pattern indicates that fixed-policy
drafting is vulnerable to acceptance/overhead imbalance and long-tail latency.
We therefore design and evaluate an adaptive-policy variant that keeps standard
target-side verification while adapting control decisions online.

Advanced variants such as EAGLE-3 and DFlash reduce drafting cost through new
drafter architectures and dedicated training pipelines, often evaluated on
strong accelerators. Our objective is different: keep a same-family
drafter/verifier stack that is deployable on consumer hardware, and recover
speed and stability by runtime control (adaptive $k$, rescue switching, and
tail fallback) rather than a newly trained drafter.

\subsection{Design objective}

Let speculative-cycle latency be
\begin{equation}
  L = \frac{T_{\text{draft}} + T_{\text{verify}}}{\tau},
\end{equation}
where $\tau$ is expected accepted tokens per cycle. In fixed-policy speculative
decoding, poorly matched samples can reduce $\tau$ and increase total verify
steps, producing heavy-tail runtime. ACSD explicitly targets this effect by
adapting draft policy at sample level.

\subsection{Derived adaptive policy template}

ACSD combines four online controls while preserving standard target-side
verification:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Adaptive $k$:} choose $k\in\{4,8,16\}$ from rolling acceptance,
        using larger blocks only when acceptance supports it. To avoid cold-start
        overshoot, each sample starts from base $k$ before adaptive updates.
  \item \textbf{Cascaded draft switch:} use 0.5B as a fast primary drafter and
        switch to 1.5B rescue drafting when acceptance remains low for
        consecutive verify steps, then enforce a cooldown before a new rescue
        episode to reduce oscillation.
  \item \textbf{Tail guardrail:} if acceptance stays below a threshold after a
        minimum generated length for multiple consecutive checks, fall back to
        target-only autoregressive decoding for the remainder of that sample.
  \item \textbf{Checkpointed execution:} save partial CSV and verify logs at
        fixed intervals so interrupted runs can resume safely.
\end{enumerate}

This design preserves verifier compatibility and enables direct comparison with
vanilla speculative decoding under the same manifests, prompts, metrics, and
hardware.

\subsection{How this guidance is used}

Future adaptive-policy evaluation should remain a strict incremental study
under the same protocol: identical target model and manifests, deterministic
and stochastic regimes, and the same metrics ($S$, $\alpha$, $B_{\text{eff}}$,
quality deltas) plus ACSD metadata (adaptive-$k$ usage, rescue usage, fallback
rate). Primary comparators are the best fixed-policy point
($k{=}4$ deterministic) and high-$k$ fixed settings ($k{=}16$).

\subsection{Observed ACSD outcomes}

Measured ACSD outcomes are reported in Section~\ref{sec:results}
(Table~\ref{tab:acsd-headline}). In brief, ACSD achieves strong acceleration
relative to autoregressive baselines in both regimes
($S{=}2.9561$ deterministic, $S{=}2.8658$ stochastic; both $p<10^{-4}$), while
keeping fallback rates low (1.6\% and 2.2\%). However, ACSD does not exceed
the fastest fixed-policy setting (0.5B, $k{=}4$ deterministic at
$S{=}3.0674$).

For deployment, adaptive control improves operational resilience (limited
fallback exposure and controlled rescue use) without retraining, but if pure
throughput is the objective on this hardware profile, the best fixed-policy
setting remains preferable.

\subsection{Scope in this paper}

This section combines design rationale with measured ACSD behavior under the
same protocol. Quantitative claims are restricted to the reported fixed-policy
and ACSD runs in this manuscript; broader generalization still requires
additional hardware/model families.
