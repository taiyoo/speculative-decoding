\begin{abstract}
\bfseries\boldmath
Speculative decoding is often reported to reduce large language model (LLM)
latency, yet gains on consumer GPUs are hardware- and runtime-dependent. We
evaluate Qwen2.5 and Qwen3 pairings on RTX4090 and RTX5090-laptop under
deterministic and stochastic policies with $k\in\{4,8,16\}$. Qwen3 reaches
$2.8548\times$ speedup on RTX4090 at deterministic $k{=}4$, but speedup falls
monotonically with $k$. Under the tested RTX5090-laptop stack, every policy is
slower than its autoregressive baseline despite preserving the relative
low-$k$ and deterministic preference. Qwen2.5 shows the same RTX4090 policy
ranking. We report time-to-first-token (TTFT), time-per-output-token (TPOT), acceptance, sample dispersion, targeted
repeat-seed runs, and Holm--Bonferroni-corrected paired tests with effect
sizes. The results show that policy ranking is more portable than absolute
speedup and that deployment claims require measurement on the target stack.
\end{abstract}
